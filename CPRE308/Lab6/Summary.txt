In this lab, I learned about three fundamental page replacement algorithms used in virtual memory management which were also discussed in lecture. Namely they are, First-In-First-Out (FIFO), Least Recently Used (LRU), and Optimal (OPT). FIFO simply replaces the oldest page in memory based on its arrival time, LRU evicts the page that hasn't been accessed for the longest time, and OPT chooses to carefully replace the page that won't be needed for the longest time in the future (based on factors). With this lab, I implemented these algorithms in C and observed how they handle page faults differently depending on the access patterns (sequential, random, locality-based).

After testing with 10,000 page accesses across the various different patterns, I discovered that OPT consistently performs the best by minimizing the page faults. However, it's impractical in real systems since future access patterns are usuallly unknown. LRU performed nearly as well as FIFO for random access but significantly better for workloads with locality of reference, demonstrating why it's commonly used in modern day operating systems. This lab reinforced my understanding from lectures about the memory management trade-offs and how your choice of the algorithm impacts the system's performance based on it's access patterns.
