1. Suppose that your friend suggests the following way to confirm that both of you have the same 
secret key. You create a random bit string which is having the length of the key, then, XOR it 
with the key, and send the result over the channel. Your partner XORs the incoming block with 
the key (which should be the same key) and sends the result back. If what you receive is your 
original string, you have verified that your partner has the same secret key, yet neither of you 
has ever transmitted the key on the channel. Is there a flaw in this scheme? 
This way seems to be secure at first glace when I worked it out on my own. Since the key is never 
really send from one person to the other, it seems like a good idea. If we say the random bit string is 
S and let the key be K. Then the first person XORs S and K to get a result (R). Then when R is sent to 
his friend, he XORs R with K to get a result S. He then sends S back to the first person who is able to 
confirm that this was his original string. 
The main flaw in using this scheme is that someone who may be sitting in the middle (Man in the 
middle) will see both the messages sent over. First he will see the R that the first person sent, then 
he will see the S that the second person sent back. Having both the R and S in his hands, the attacker 
(if he is smart enough, and he knows what you are doing) simply has to XOR R and S together to 
retreive the K that you and your partner have been using. XOR is an associative and commutative 
function which is the reason why this method is actually weak.
